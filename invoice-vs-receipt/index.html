Invoice vs receipt: what's the difference?

An invoice asks your client to pay. A receipt confirms that they already did. They look alike, but they happen at different moments and do different jobs.

InvoiceReceipt
WhenBefore paymentAfter payment
PurposeRequests paymentProves payment
Key datesIssue date and due dateDate the money was received
ShowsBalance due and how to payAmount paid and payment method
NumberInvoice number, like INV-0042Receipt number, often linked to the invoice

When to send an invoice

Send an invoice when you have delivered work or goods and expect to be paid later. It gives your client a due date and the details they need to pay, and gives you a record for your accounts.

When to send a receipt

Send a receipt when a client pays you, especially if they paid in cash, on the spot, or asked for proof for their own records or expenses.

Questions

Is a paid invoice the same as a receipt?

Often it can serve as one. If you mark an invoice as paid and it shows the amount and date received, many clients accept it as proof of payment. Some businesses still prefer a separate receipt.

Do I need to send both?

Send an invoice to ask for payment. Send a receipt when the client asks for proof, or when they paid on the spot without an invoice.
